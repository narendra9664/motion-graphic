# Long-form script (6-30 min)

Core law: **reality must beat expectations**. The title sets the expectation; the first lines must confirm it
(click confirmation) and then raise it; every section must deliver something the viewer did not already know.
Viewers decide in 5-second, 30-second and 2-minute windows: half of them leave in the first 30 seconds of a weak intro.

## 0. Inputs (ask only for what is missing)
Topic, target viewer and what they already know, length, goal (views / subs / a product to promote), narrator voice,
any house rules or style bible in the project (read it first; its rules win).

## 1. Packaging (before any writing)
- **Idea**: one sentence naming the pain point or the rabbit hole. Weak idea = stop.
- **Title**: triggers a curiosity loop and sets a precise expectation. Write 5 options; pick the one whose promise
  you can beat. Note the exact expectation it creates.
- **Thumbnail**: a loose concept is enough to start; refine later.
- **Story lens**: list the obvious angles every other channel will take, then choose a less common one (the business
  mechanism, the overlooked decision, the number, the villain nobody names, the "it was in plain sight" angle).

## 2. Research + fact discipline
- Build a fact bank: fact | source | date | tier (A primary/major outlet, B trade press, C single-source). 30-60 facts for
  a 10-minute script. Quotes verbatim only, with the source.
- Real people: allegations attributed and denials included; no crime stated as fact without a conviction; mark what is
  disputed. Keep a "do not use" list of myths you found.

## 3. Outline gate (bullets first, script later)
- Bullet every body point. For each: **what** it is, **why** it matters, **how** it fits the whole story.
- **Uniqueness test**: is each point new, better distilled, or better illustrated than what the viewer already knows?
  If not, research more. Do not write a summary of common knowledge.
- **Order for climbing value**: the second-best point first, the best second, then the rest descending, or build to a
  final peak. Never best-first-then-worse: it teaches the viewer that value is decaying.
- Write the **ending first**: the final insight and the last line, then fill the middle.

## 4. The intro (first 30-60 s): five Lego bricks
1. **Context / click confirmation**: say bluntly what the video is about, matching the title (what + why first,
   who/how next, when/where last).
2. **Common belief**: state what the viewer already believes about it (builds common ground and sets up contrast).
3. **Contrarian take**: "but that's not what happened" / "there is actually a pattern". This is the first
   expectation-beat.
4. **Proof**: why trust this (sources, numbers, access, the research behind it).
5. **Plan**: the roadmap (three decisions, five turning points, two near-deaths), so the viewer can see where it goes.
Plus a **visual hook** in the first frames, and stakes at 70/100 intensity from line one.

### Alternative intro: the dilemma opener (great for strategy/ethics/"what would you do" stories)
Drop the viewer into a concrete choice in second person ("Suppose you're the CEO, and you have 30 days of payroll…").
Ask what they would do. Then give a variant that flips their intuition, so they feel the tension between two
principles before you name either. Name the principles only after they have felt them. This makes the familiar
strange: it does not add information so much as a new way of seeing, and it implicates the viewer personally.

## 5. The body
Each point runs a **value loop**:
- **Context**: what it is, said simply.
- **Application**: how it happened / how to use it, with the most vivid concrete example you have.
- **Framing**: why it matters and how it fits the bigger story (zoom out, so the viewer sees why to keep watching).
Alternate zoom-in (tactic, scene, detail) and zoom-out (big picture) like a wave.

Inside every section, run the **addiction loop**: stakes (character + risk + urgency) -> a specific, guessable big
question -> a head fake (surprising AND logical; plant the clues) -> a re-hook that opens the next loop in the same
breath. Join beats with **"but"** or **"therefore"**, never "and then".

**Modern arc**: open at 70/100, first major conflict within the first 1-2 minutes (spike to ~90), release, then a new
peak every 2-5 minutes. The single biggest peak is either first (then each later peak lower) or last (each peak
building). Never a slow classic bell curve.

**Re-hook every seam** (the end of a section is where viewers leave): "that would have saved them, except…", "but
that fix created a bigger problem", "and that's when the real trouble started". Make the viewer re-buy in with new
stakes before the old ones are fully released.

**Story tools**: a villain or contrast (a rival, Wall Street, physics, time); something to root for (make the protagonist
relatable via a humble origin, a flaw, a loss); real-case storytelling with sensory details and one deadpan line
from the record; steelman both sides of any controversy, then let the evidence decide; humour from real facts.

**Line craft**: ~150-165 words per minute for narration. One sentence per line, varied lengths (jagged right edge).
Conversational: write it like a voice note to one friend. Say hard ideas twice: once plainly, once as an everyday
analogy a child would get. Every line gets a visual; never narrate what the picture already shows.

## 6. Native embeds (sponsors, your own app, a free resource)
Plan them in the outline: find the point whose pain the product truly solves, frame the pain, offer the product as
the solve in 1-3 lines, and continue without breaking flow. Or speed-run a dense framework and offer the resource as
the recap. Never in the first 60 s. Max one per ~5 minutes. Sell the outcome/identity, not features.

## 7. Outro
End on a high note: compress the journey into the 3 things they now know, restate the pain point you solved (the
"free chocolate with the bill"), land the final insight in one shareable sentence, and open a loop into the next
video or the series ("next time: the decision that almost killed them a second time"). No long goodbye.

## 8. Output format
1. **Package**: idea, 5 titles (pick + expectation), thumbnail concept, story lens vs the obvious lenses.
2. **Fact bank** with tiers + "do not use" list.
3. **Outline**: points with what/why/how, ordered by value (mark #2-best first, best second), ending written first.
4. **Script**: sections with timestamps; per section a table: # | line | beat (stakes / question / head fake /
   re-hook / value loop part) | visual | music/SFX cue. Mark every native embed.
5. **Retention map**: where each loop opens and closes, peak timings (should recur every 2-5 min).
6. **Self-check** (1-5 each, rewrite anything under 4): click confirmation in 2 lines? contrarian take by 30 s?
   first point stronger than anything the viewer knew? a re-hook at every seam? only but/therefore joins? every claim
   sourced, every allegation attributed? one-sentence takeaway? outro opens the next video?
7. Ask for approval before voice or production.
