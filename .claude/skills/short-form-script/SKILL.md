# Short-form script (30-90 s)

The job: a viewer who did not ask for this video stays to the last word, then replays or shares it.
Everything below serves one law: **reality must beat expectations** at every moment. Expectations come from the
first frame and title; reality is what each next line delivers. When reality wins they stay. When it loses they scroll.

## 0. Inputs to collect (ask only for what is missing)
- Topic or subject (person, company, event). Target viewer (who, what they care about). Platform + length.
- Goal: views, follows, or a promotion (an app/product to embed natively). Voice: narrator style (energy, pace).
- House rules (e.g. no captions, English only, one steady voice). If a project has a SKILL.md or STYLE_BIBLE.md,
  read it first; its rules win.

## 1. Research and facts (non-negotiable for real people)
- Collect 8-15 facts with a source and date each. Prefer primary/major outlets. Mark single-source claims.
- Allegations about real people are always **attributed** ("officials believed", "reports say"), and the denial is
  included when one exists. Never invent quotes. Never state a crime as fact without a conviction.
- Hunt for the **surprising true detail**: a number, a quote, an irony, an absurd coincidence. Jokes must be real facts.

## 2. Packaging before writing
- **Idea in one sentence**: the curiosity gap or pain point. If it is not interesting in one sentence, stop and re-research.
- **Story lens** (your unique angle). List the 3 lenses everyone else will use, then pick a less common one:
  the business impact, the overlooked person, the number nobody mentions, the contrarian reading, the "it was hiding in
  plain sight" angle. Aim to be a category of one.
- **Title / on-screen premise**: it sets the expectation the first line must confirm (click confirmation) and then beat.
- **Format steal**: borrow a format the brain already likes (rise-and-fall, "how X actually works", "the one decision
  that…", "X did Y and it backfired"). Familiar format + new content beats an original format.

## 3. Outline from the END backwards
1. Write the **last line first** ("the last dab"): the line someone would repeat to a friend. For a loop ending, the
   last line must flow grammatically into the first line so a replay sounds like one sentence.
2. Write the **first line**.
3. Fill the middle with 4-7 beats joined only by **"but"** or **"therefore"** (never "and then"). If two beats are
   joined by "and then", cut one or add a complication.
4. Uniqueness gut-check: would the target viewer already know each beat? Replace any common-knowledge beat with a
   sharper fact or a better example. Put your **second-best** surprising fact first in the body and the **best** one
   second, so the value climbs.

## 4. The hook (first 1-3 s), stacked in this order
- **What + why it matters** first, **who/how** second, **when/where** last (never open on scene-setting).
- Punchy and plot-indicative: the first line tells you what the video is about. Never "wait till you see this".
- A **paradox stated as fact**, a number, or a "they did X, and it cost them Y". 8-16 words.
- **Visual hook**: the first frame shows what the line says (visuals are read before audio). Note it in the table.
- Add a **credential or stake** within 2 s when you can (the richest man in X, a $5 trillion company, 74 soldiers).
- **Pivot right after the hook**: line 2 moves straight into the mechanism or the stakes ("Because…", "Here's why…").
  House rule: **never use the phrase "You see"** in any script.
- The three filters the brain runs in half a second: have I liked this kind of thing before? is something unexpected
  happening? is this about me or my life? Hit at least two.

## 5. The engine: dopamine ladder + addiction loop
Tag every line with one of these beats. A good short climbs the whole ladder.
1. **Stimulation** (0-2 s): motion, colour, contrast in the first frame. Write it into the visual column.
2. **Captivation**: plant one specific open question (asked or implied). Specific enough that the viewer can guess.
3. **Anticipation**: feed facts that let them predict the answer; the dopamine peak is just before the reveal.
4. **Validation**: close the loop with a non-obvious but logical answer.
5. **Affection**: humanise the protagonist (a humble origin, a quirk, a loss, a kindness). Faceless channels earn
   affection through a recurring narrator voice, a recurring visual device and a consistent world.
6. **Revelation**: the takeaway that makes the viewer smarter (and makes the channel a reliable source).
7. **Loop ending**: last line sets up the first.

Inside the body, run the **addiction loop** on repeat:
- **Stakes**: a character to root for + something at risk + urgency (a clock, a deadline, a number running out).
- **Big question**: specific, guessable.
- **Head fake**: an answer that breaks the prediction but clicks instantly (clues were planted). Random = confusing.
- **Re-hook**: open the next loop in the same breath the last one closes. Connective tissue: "which would have been
  great, except…", "but here's the problem…", "and that's when…", "which is exactly why…".
- Never leave a gap where the viewer could think "okay, done". Shorter time between hits = longer watch time.
- Add a **villain or contrast** (a rival, Wall Street, a rule, time itself) and give the viewer **something to root for**.

## 6. Line craft
- ~3.6-4.0 words/s spoken: 55 s = ~200-220 words; 65 s = ~240 words. One idea per line.
- **Rhythm**: vary sentence length: short. Medium ones. Then one long line that builds. Write each sentence on its own
  line; the right edge should look jagged.
- **Tone**: talk to one friend. Contractions, plain words, present tense for story mode. No jargon unless explained
  in the next clause.
- **Atomic shareability**: the core idea must fit in one retellable sentence. For a hard idea, say it twice: once
  plainly, once as a child-level analogy ("Ever caught a yawn just by watching someone yawn?").
- **Show, don't narrate the obvious**: never describe what the picture already shows. Every line gets a literal visual.
- **Every character is alive, not just the lead.** Write a reaction for the extras on every beat (new expression +
  a bounce/hop: surprise, nod, flinch, cheer), and note it in the visual column. Background characters never stand
  frozen: they breathe, sway, glance and tilt. Bounce and squash-and-stretch on reactions is what makes the world feel real.
- Deadpan double lines and real-fact jokes land well ("Luckily… / Unluckily…").
- Share trigger: ask "what does the sharer get to say about themselves?" Humour and surprise spread; anger rarely.

## 7. Native promotion (when promoting an app/product)
- Never lead with the product. Find the beat whose pain point the product actually solves, frame the pain, show the
  product as the natural fix in one line, move on. It should feel like part of the story, not an ad break.
- Sell the identity/outcome (means-end chain: feature -> what it does -> who it makes me), not the feature list.
- One embed per short at most; place it after the first payoff (~60-70% through), never in the hook.

## 8. Output format
1. **Package**: idea sentence, story lens (and the 3 common lenses it beats), 3 title options, thumbnail/first-frame concept.
2. **Fact sheet**: fact | source | date | tier (A/B/C) | how it is worded on air.
3. **Script table**: # | line | words | beat (ladder/loop tag) | visual (what is on screen) | SFX/music cue.
4. **Totals**: word count, estimated runtime, where each loop opens and closes.
5. **Self-check** (score each 1-5, rewrite anything under 4):
   - Hook confirms the premise in line 1 and beats it by line 2?
   - Specific big question by ~3 s? Head fake that is surprising AND logical?
   - Only "but/therefore" joins? Re-hook at every section seam?
   - Pivot into the mechanism right after the hook (and no "You see" anywhere)? Every character (not just the lead) reacting with expression + bounce?
   - Rhythm varied? Every line visual? Nothing narrates the obvious?
   - One-sentence shareable takeaway? Last line loops into the first?
   - Every claim sourced; every allegation attributed; no defamation risk?
6. Ask for approval before voice or production.

## Reference: modern arc for shorts
Start at 70/100 intensity (no slow build), first conflict spike within ~5-8 s, then quick release/re-spike cycles
every ~8-12 s, biggest twist in the last third, end on the loop line. The classic slow bell-curve arc loses most
viewers before the rising action.
